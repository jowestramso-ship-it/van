Attachment points. Each white dot on the drawing is a place where the strip is fixed to the van. On S1, S2, S3, S7, and S8 a dot is an adhesive pad (structural adhesive or VHB tape) straight onto the steel, placed on solid steel and kept clear of the holes. On S4 and S6 a dot is a 3 5/8 in spacer block bonded to the 1 1/2 in strip of steel, with the strip screwed to the block. On S5 a dot is a spacer block bonded onto a rib, so S5 touches the van only at the ten ribs, 13 to 16 in apart, and spans the pans between them. This is general van-build practice, so test the adhesive bond on your own steel before you rely on it.

Rough materials, counting 154 in per strip with S1 stopping at the wheel well: about 100 ft of 3/4 x 1 1/2 in strip. Spacers are needed under S4, S5, and S6 at every rib, about 10 per strip. This is a starting layout from your measurements, so check the strip positions against where you want shelves, cabinets, and outlets before you glue anything.

Reading the wall

  • Pans (light gray): eleven pans, each 25.5 in tall (your measurement), from 31 to 56.5 in up (P1's top is 10.5 in below the bottom of T2). The pan is the body of the van; the other side of the metal is the outside, so a hole through a pan goes through to the outside.
  • Ribs (light gray): the 3 in strips between the pans, standing 1/8 in off the body. That is the only standoff measured so far, and it is the shallow space you have behind anything you mount on a rib.
  • Flat steel: a band above the pans (about 56 to 63 in), the belt line below them (about 22 to 31 in, with five oval slots at about 27 in), and the seam pillar at about 98 in. How far these stand off the body is not measured yet; that is the next thing to find.
  • Holes (light gray inside): eight rectangular cutouts and a round hole low on the wall, the pillar opening, and the openings in the roof structure above the wall. These are where the inner steel is missing. The light gray you see in every hole is the outside wall of the van (the body), at the depth listed, the same as the pans. The depth numbers are for the steel around each hole, not inside it: around the top holes (T1-T10 and T8a) it is 3 in from the body at the top edge and 3 3/4 in at the bottom edge; around the L and R holes in the lower wall it is 3 3/4 in; inside every hole you see the body itself. The slots (S), the pillar opening and the low pocket are not measured yet.

E3. Shower curtains on three sides, with the pan tipping up

Two flexible plastic curtains, like a home shower curtain, hang free from a rod at the ceiling on three sides, and slide around on it (left: looking down at the 36 x 24 in pan). The rod runs 1 1/2 in inside the pan's edge, 33 in across the front and 22 1/2 in down each end, and the two curtains overlap 6 in in the middle of the front. The only rod is the one at the ceiling; there is no rod in the hem: the bottom is free, with small weights (a light stainless chain or sewn-in weights) that keep it inside the 3 in curb in use. Right: from the rear, along the wall.

Folding the pan: pull both curtains to the sides, so they lie in the pan. Then tip the pan up toward the wall. Because the curtains are loose plastic, nothing blocks the pan: it carries the hems with it and they fold into the gap between the pan and the wall, and the rest hangs from the ceiling rod against the folded pan. The pan stands 24 in tall and about 3 1/2 in thick, and its top (24 1/2 in) stays 6 1/2 in below the shower nook (31 in). The drawing of the folded curtain is a sketch; plastic drapes however it falls.

Things to watch: (1) no stiff rod in the hem, as it would jam between the pan and the wall; (2) the rod runs to the wall so the curtain seals the ends in use, which means the parked curtain is pinched against the wall when the pan is up; if you stop the rod about 5 in short of the wall instead, the curtain is not pinched, but each back corner then has a 5 in gap where spray can escape; (3) wipe the pan and curtains dry before folding, or the pinched plastic mildews; (4) keep the lowest few inches of the curtain away from the hinge and the drain hose; (5) hold the gathered curtains with a loop or magnet at the ceiling while driving. Total fabric: about 80 to 100 in wide by 74 in long, as two curtains (each about 40 to 50 in wide), cut from standard 72 in curtains.
