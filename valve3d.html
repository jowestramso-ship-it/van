The shower valve behind a small door, in 3D

The shower back wall at the cab end, seen from inside the stall (cab on the left). The back wall is one piece, with a small door over the valve: the valve is mounted on the door, which swings open like a cabinet door and shows the two swivel elbows and the lines behind it. Drag to look around, scroll or pinch to zoom, and open the door.

Drawn from the plan: the shower back wall is the 3/8 in plywood wall on the furring rails (rails S3 to S6 at 21 1/4, 30 1/4, 43 1/2 and 57 1/4 in up, 3/4 in thick, standing 3 5/8 in off the steel on the spacer blocks; only the two spacer blocks of S4 near the valve are drawn) with the PVC lining glued on it. The lining here is one 4 x 8 ft PVC sheet (drawn 1/8 in; the sheet is not chosen: measure yours), not planks. The door does not hang on the thin PVC: the opening runs the whole gap between rails S4 and S5 (32 to 41 3/4 in up, 25 to 33 in along) through the plywood and the PVC, so the wall around its top and bottom edges is backed by those rails, and a wood jamb (3/4 in by 1 1/2 in, screwed to both rails) stands behind the plywood on each side. The hinge, on the cab side, and the four screws or quarter-turn latches go through the wall into that frame. The door is a rigid 1/2 in board (PVC board, or plywood skinned with the PVC), 9 x 10 3/4 in, overlapping the opening by 1/2 in on a gasket. The valve is at 29 in along and 36 in up on the door, two handles with the hot and cold inlets 4 in apart. How deep the valve sits behind the door and the size of the elbows (two SharkBite UC532LFA) are my estimates: check them with your valve in hand. The cold side of the valve is the rear one (on the right here) and the hot the cab one (on the left): an assumption, check the valve. The hot line, dropping at 31 in along, passes through the S4 spacer block at 29 1/2 to 33 in, which gets a 3/4 in hole drilled through it. Leave 8 to 12 in of extra PEX behind the valve, loose, so the door can swing open on its lines.
