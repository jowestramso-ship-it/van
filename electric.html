Electrical and lighting layout

A proposal for where the electrics go, drawn on the same steel and furring rails as the wall marking map, with the furniture outlined for reference. Positions are inches from the back mark (0 is 6 in in from the rear-door edge) and up from the floor; the ceiling plan uses inches from the rear-door edge. The steel and furring rails are the measured drawing; the electrical items are my proposal, so check each one against the real wall and tell me what to move.

What you chose: a 2000 W inverter, charging from the alternator and from shore power (no solar), a 120 V fridge, and the water pump under the sink. What I assumed (tell me if wrong):
  • The battery is lithium, 280 Ah at 12.8 V (about 3.6 kWh), where the plan has it under the couch, 52 to 73 in.
  • Your equipment: a BELTTT 2000 W inverter (an inverter only: the listings show no charger and no transfer switch), a LiTime 60 A DC-DC charger from the alternator (2 AWG from the starter battery), and a VEVOR 80 A lithium shore charger. All three sit on one pull-out gear shelf over the battery under the couch (the shelf slides out into the aisle on two 18 in slides; every cable to it has a loop for that, see the cable table). Because the inverter has no transfer switch, the AC panel has a 30 A transfer switch that connects either shore power or the inverter, never both, and the shore charger is fed from the shore side only, on its own 15 A breaker (about 11 A at 120 V). With the charger running, a heater or microwave and the fridge could pass the 30 A shore inlet, so the charger is its own circuit and the total is worth watching.
  • Ceiling lights: four: one over the shower (wet rated) and one over the countertop and sink together, both on the slat at 58 in across, and two in the center row (36 and about 100 from the rear-door edge, centered across the van; each is kept 2 in clear of a rib, so they sit at 35 7/8 and 102 1/8), all 12 V and on a slat. The two in the center row share one switch on the passenger wall just rear of the sink. Everything else is lit from the cabinets, the jar nook and the soft LED strip.
  • Your 12 V lights: a 6 pack of recessed 12 V LED lights for RVs, boats and campers (3000 K warm white, CRI 90+, stepless dimming). The plan has eight 12 V lights (four in the ceiling and four under the cabinets), and you have bought enough of them to cover all eight. I have not seen the listing’s numbers, so check these before you cut any hole: the cutout (hole) size and the depth behind the panel (the ceiling is 3/16 plywood plus a 3/4 slat; the cabinet bottoms are 3/4, and the cabinet is open above them); the watts each (I assumed 4 W); whether the dimming is built into the light (a touch ring or a press-and-hold) or needs a dimmer switch, because the rocker switches in this plan are plain on-off; and the IP rating, since the light over the shower has to be wet rated (IP65). Send me those numbers and I will change the plan to match.
  • Your 12 V fuse block: a 10-way blade fuse box with a blown-fuse LED on each way and a waterproof cover. The plan uses five of its ten ways, each with its own wire: 1 the lights (WR16, 5 A), 2 the soft LED strip (WR14, 10 A, with the dimming controller), 3 the roof fan (WR15, 5 A), 4 the water pump (WR31, 10 A), 5 the control panel (WR32, 3 A); ways 6 to 10 are spare (for example to give the ceiling lights and the cabinet lights a fuse each). It is fed by WR13, 10 AWG with a 30 A fuse at the bus. The A/C is not on it (its own 100 A fuse at the battery). I have not seen the listing, so check these before you buy fuses and mount it: that it takes standard ATC or ATO blade fuses (not mini); its rating for each way (most are 30 A, fine for these) and for the feed (most are 100 A in); that its input terminal takes a 10 AWG ring lug; and whether it has a negative bus bar (most have only the + side: then the five − wires go to the − bus bar next to it, not onto the block). Its wire terminals are screws or clamps for thicker wire than 18 AWG: use ferrules or ring lugs on the fine-strand copper wire.
  • Your transfer switch: a TS-30, 30 A. That name is the Go Power! model (an automatic switch with a shore input, a second input for a generator or here the inverter, and one output, locked so that only one input is connected; the listing says it prefers the generator input and waits 20 to 45 seconds before switching to it); other makers use the same name, so check the label. Shore goes to the shore input (WR7, 10 AWG), the inverter output goes to the other input (WR8), and the output goes to the AC panel (WR9, 10 AWG). Things to check in its manual: (1) the priority: if it prefers the second input, then whenever the inverter is on the loads come from the inverter, not shore, so switch the inverter off when you want shore power (the shore charger is fed from the shore side anyway); (2) whether it switches the neutral as well as the hot: this decides the one neutral-to-ground bond (see the note under the one-line diagram); (3) the wire size its terminals take (10 AWG is planned). Its size is listed as about 9 5/8 x 7 1/4 x 4 3/4 in: the hub board is drawn only 10 in tall (1 to 11 in up) and also has to hold the bus bars, the shunt, the Class T fuse, the fuse block and the AC panel, so it will not all fit as drawn: the hub board needs a layout with the real sizes.

These are estimates to size the system, not a measurement.

7. Order of work

  • Before the walls and ceiling are covered: run the risers, the cables across the ceiling and every branch, with grommets in all steel and the 12 V and 120 V in separate looms (at least 2 in apart, crossing at right angles). This is the rough-in step of the build order.
  • With the furring rails: fix the electrical boxes to blocking between the furring rails, and add the blocking behind the hub board, the switch plates and the outlets before the planks go on.
  • Last: the hub board and fuses, the inverter and the two chargers, the transfer switch, the lights and the plates, after the covering is up.
  • Test every circuit with a meter before you connect the battery, and test the GFCI outlets with the button.
