Electrical and lighting layout

A proposal for where the electrics go, drawn on the same steel and furring rails as the wall marking map, with the furniture outlined for reference. Positions are inches from the back mark (0 is 6 in in from the rear-door edge) and up from the floor; the ceiling plan uses inches from the rear-door edge. The steel and furring rails are the measured drawing; the electrical items are my proposal, so check each one against the real wall and tell me what to move.

What you chose: a 2000 W inverter, charging from the alternator and from shore power (no solar), a 120 V fridge, and the water pump under the sink. What I assumed (tell me if wrong):
  • The battery is lithium, 280 Ah at 12.8 V (about 3.6 kWh), where the plan has it under the couch, 52 to 73 in.
  • Your equipment: a BELTTT 2000 W inverter (an inverter only: the listings show no charger and no transfer switch), a LiTime 60 A DC-DC charger from the alternator (2 AWG from the starter battery), and a VEVOR 80 A lithium shore charger. All three sit on one pull-out gear shelf over the battery under the couch (the shelf slides out into the aisle on two 18 in slides; every cable to it has a loop for that, see the cable table). Because the inverter has no transfer switch, the AC panel has a 30 A transfer switch that connects either shore power or the inverter, never both, and the shore charger is fed from the shore side only, on its own 15 A breaker (about 11 A at 120 V). With the charger running, a heater or microwave and the fridge could pass the 30 A shore inlet, so the charger is its own circuit and the total is worth watching.
  • Ceiling lights: only two, one over the shower (wet rated) and one over the countertop and sink together, both on one slat and 2 in clear of the ribs. Everything else is lit from the cabinets, the jar nook and the soft LED strip.
  • Light switches: by the couch (the light under the cabinet over the middle of the couch), by the dinette on the cab wall (the two lights under the cabinet over the seats, the ceiling light over the countertop and the sink), a small plate at the rear doors (roof fan and the shower light), and the dimmer for the soft strip. Each switch runs its own group, so ordinary 12 V rated switches work: no push buttons or controller are needed.
  • Under-cabinet lights: one in the middle of the couch, and two over the dinette, each centered over its bench seat and then moved 6 in toward the table, all on the underside of the wall cabinets. They replace the under-cabinet LED strips, the wall reading lights and the single light over the table I had first suggested.
  • Soft LED lighting: one warm white dimmable strip around the ceiling edge, along the driver side, across the back and along the passenger side (154 + 68 + 154 = 376 in, about 31 ft), in three runs fed separately so none is longer than about 13 ft from its feed. At about 3 W a foot that is about 94 W (8 A) at full, so it has its own 10 A circuit. One switch with a dimmer next to the couch plate.
  • Outlets: the four 120 V outlets you asked for (couch, over the middle of the dinette table, under the TV, behind the fridge), plus one for the microwave, which cannot share a circuit with them. Everything else is 12 V: the lights, the fan, the pump and the control panel. There are no USB-C or 12 V sockets (you said they are not needed); phones charge from the 120 V outlets.
  • Wet bath: a wet-rated (IP65) ceiling light over the stall (the exact center is under a rib, so it sits 2 1/2 in toward the cab), and no outlet in the stall (you said no outlet there). The light is at the center of the stall (18 in along the wall, 56 in across from the driver wall). That point falls in the 1 in gap between two slats, so mount the light on a short cross block laid across the gap, or on the nearest slat 2 in to one side. It takes the place of the plan’s first passenger-side light, so there are still six lights.
  • The hot water heater (1440 W) and the microwave (about 1500 W) must not run together: that is more than a 2000 W inverter can supply. A 2 pole, 3 position selector switch (off, microwave, heater) makes it impossible: see item EP9 on the passenger wall.
  • All 120 V work is for an electrician to check; I give layout and sizes, not a code review.

Buy in total: about 10 ft red and 10 ft black of 2/0, about 32 ft red and 32 ft black of 2 AWG, about 15 ft red and 15 ft black of 6 AWG (or 4), and 4 ft of 4 AWG black. The drop is the voltage lost in the copper at the full current, one way out and back, from 20 C copper resistance; keep it near 3% for the charger runs and well under that for the short ones.

  • Cable: tinned copper, fine strand (class K or better, UL 1426 or marine battery cable, 105 C insulation), true AWG with no copper-clad aluminium; red and black. Welding cable is fine if it is tinned or the ends are sealed.
  • Lugs: tinned copper ring lugs, one per cable end (about 10 in 2/0, 16 in 2 AWG, 6 in 6 AWG, 2 in 4 AWG), each with the hole size of the stud it goes on (the inverter’s, the battery post, the fuse block and the bus bar: check each before buying), crimped with a hydraulic or hammer crimper, then adhesive-lined heat shrink. Do not solder high-current lugs.
  • Fuses and gear: a Class T holder and 250 A fuse, a 500 A shunt, two bus bars rated 250 A or more, an 80 A fuse and holder at the starter battery, an 80 A and a 100 A for the chargers at the bus, and a 100 A for the A/C at the battery; a cable cutter and a hydraulic crimper (or have a shop crimp the lugs).

That is 1.5 times the battery on a hot day with the A/C on for 8 hours, so it needs refilling every day: driving through the alternator charger (the 60 A DC-DC is about 800 W, so 2 hours adds about 1.6 kWh) or shore power (the VEVOR 80 A is about 1.1 kW, so an hour adds about 1.1 kWh). 6. Order of work

  • Before the walls and ceiling are covered: run the risers, the ceiling trunk and every branch, with grommets in all steel and the 12 V and 120 V in separate looms (at least 2 in apart, crossing at right angles). This is the rough-in step of the build order.
  • With the furring rails: fix the electrical boxes to blocking between the furring rails, and add the blocking behind the hub board, the switch plates and the outlets before the planks go on.
  • Last: the hub board and fuses, the inverter and the two chargers, the transfer switch, the lights and the plates, after the covering is up.
  • Test every circuit with a meter before you connect the battery, and test the GFCI outlets with the button.
