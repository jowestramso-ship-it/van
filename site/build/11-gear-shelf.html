C4. Driver side: the gear shelf over the battery, how to make it

The inverter, the DC-DC charger and the shore charger all sit on one tray over the battery. The tray is 1/2 in plywood between two 3/4 x 2 1/2 in end rails, on two 18 in full-extension slides: one on the closet's end panel (74 in from the back) and one on a plywood divider just west of the wheel well. Its plain face is flush with the couch front, and nothing else covers the front of the couch under the deck, so you pull the face and the whole shelf comes out 18 in into the aisle, with every unit and terminal open from above. The battery stays on the floor under it, with a 1 in gap for its terminals and lugs. The stack, from the floor: battery 9 1/2, gap 1, tray 1/2, the tallest unit (the DC-DC) 4 1/2, air 1/2, which is the 16 in under the deck; that is why the couch was raised 3 1/2 in. The front rail of the couch lifts out across the opening, because the units are taller than the 1 1/2 in under it; it is a steel tube, not wood (diagram C5 shows how to make it).

How to build it, in order:

  1. Raise the couch first. The base (two end panels, the wall ledger and the front rail) goes up 3 1/2 in: deck 16 to 16 3/4 up, the folded leaf on it to 17 1/2, seat top 22 1/2. The leg feet screw out another 3 1/2 in (diagram C3).
  2. Stand the divider. Cut it 3/4 x 16 x 26 (26 in deep, so its front edge is flush with the face), clamp it plumb at 38 5/8 in from the back, and screw it to the floor cleat and the cleat under the deck; also check it is parallel to the closet end panel (74 in) within 1/16 in over its length, because the slides must run parallel.
  3. Mark the slide height with a story stick. The tray bottom sits 10 1/2 in up (1 in over the battery top). Each slide is 1 3/4 in tall and centered on the rail, so its center is 11 3/4 in up and its top 12 5/8. Screw the fixed members to the divider face and the closet end panel (with 5/8 in screws, so they do not come out through the 3/4 in plywood), their front ends flush with the couch front, both at the same height.
  4. Build the tray. Cut the plywood and the two rails, glue and screw the rails to the ends of the tray with the bottoms flush, glue on the rear lip, and check the diagonals match to 1/16 in. The tray with its rails is 33 5/16 wide, so it leaves 5/8 in each side for a 1/2 in slide and about 1/8 in of clearance.
  5. Screw the moving members to the outer faces of the rails, with the tray upside down on a flat bench, then set the tray on the fixed members and push it home. If the slides bind, shim the divider end by 1/16 in or loosen the screws and re-seat the members; do not force them.
  6. Fit the face to the front of the tray and the rails, flush with the couch front (25 1/4 to 26 in out from the wall), from 10 1/4 to 14 1/4 in up; the lift-out rail goes above it (14 1/2 to 16 in up; how to build it is diagram C5) ; nothing covers the couch front below the face, so the battery and the lawn chairs stay in view and in reach.
  7. Set the gear on the tray. Rear row: the DC-DC charger at 41 1/16 to 57 1/16 in along the wall and the inverter at 57 9/16 to 72 3/8; front row: the shore charger at 41 1/16 to 50 9/16; 1/2 in between the units, 1/4 in from the rails and the face. Hold each down through its mounting ears (the 4 1/2 in DC-DC leaves 1/2 in under the deck). Keep the inverter's fan side facing the open front, not the divider or the closet panel.
  8. Wire it with loops. Every cable from the battery and the starter battery, the chargers' AC inputs and the inverter's AC output runs over the rear lip to a U-shaped loop 24 in longer than a fixed run (2/0 and 2 AWG cable bends about 4 in tight, so make the loop 8 in deep or more), tied down along the closet end panel and the tray rail with clamps so it cannot catch the slide. Fuses stay fixed on the battery side: the Class T within 7 in of the post.
  9. Test it: lift out the front rail, pull the face, watch the loops, check the shelf clears the fridge by 3 in, then push it home. Label the lift-out rail "LIFT OUT BEFORE PULLING THE SHELF". About 28 lb loaded: check the sag at full extension (it should be well under 1/8 in with the units on).

Not settled: the exact slide model (check its thickness and height against the 1/2 in and 1 3/4 in drawn here), the battery's real terminal height (1 in of gap assumes right-angle lugs), the real weights of the two chargers (28 lb is a guess apart from the inverter's 7.7 lb), where the cables enter the closet end panel, and the front rail's support at its notches. The couch is now 3 1/2 in higher and the cushions are 5 in (they were 6): a seated 6 ft head is 1 1/2 in under the wall cabinets, tight but not touching.
