Step 13 of 18
Shower pan: set it, drain it, line the stall

Passenger side, rear end: after the walls and W3 are framed and the subfloor is down, before the toilet and the nook.

You bought the pan (36 x 24 in), so this page is about setting it, not building it: bed it, drain it through the floor at the rear-door end, test it, then line the plywood of the stall wall and W3 with a thin waterproof FRP panel and seal every joint with flexible sealant. Three things are guesses until you measure: where the drain hole is on the pan (4 in along and 12 out is mine), how tall the pan's lip is against the wall, and which liner product you pick (FRP is my choice, not yours). Change them in the plan's sizes and the drawings and checks follow.

Steps, in order

  1. Measure the pan before anything is cut. Write down its length and depth (36 x 24), the height of its curb and of its lip against the wall, the size of the drain hole and how far it is from the rear end and from the wall (you said the middle of the rear-door end; the plan has it at 4 in along and 12 out, which is my guess). Put the real numbers into the plan's sizes (Shower drain hole, Liner), so the drawings and checks follow.
  2. Look under the floor where the hole goes. Cross-members, wires, brake or fuel lines and the grey tank's place all matter before you drill. The hole at the middle of the rear-door end is near the rear bumper area: check it clears the frame.
  3. Check and prepare the floor. The subfloor under the pan must be flat to 1/8 in over the 36 x 24 in (a 4 ft level or a straightedge). Fill any dip with a flexible leveling filler or shim it; the pan's floor is thin and a fiberglass or acrylic pan must be supported all over, or it cracks where you stand.
  4. Drill the drain hole. Dry-fit the pan, mark the center of its drain hole on the floor, take the pan out, drill a pilot hole through the subfloor and the steel, then cut with a hole saw 1/4 in bigger than the drain body. Deburr the steel edge, treat it with rust converter and epoxy primer, and seal the cut edge of the subfloor with epoxy. Vacuum all the chips out of the van.
  5. Bed the pan. Run zig-zag beads of the flexible adhesive over the subfloor about every 3 in (keep it 2 in back from the hole), bed the drain flange in neutral silicone on the pan, set the pan down against the plywood wall and a hair short of W3, and press it. Check it is level side to side and falls to the drain. Weigh it down (jugs of water, 100 lb) for the adhesive's open time and wipe the squeeze-out.
  6. Connect the drain from below: gasket and lock nut on the body, then the tailpiece, the P-trap and the hose to the grey tank, with a vent on the tank. Keep the hose falling all the way to the tank.
  7. Seal the pan to the wall. A bead of flexible sealant where the pan lip meets the plywood, and the plywood edge below the liner sealed with epoxy (drawing B).
  8. Fill test. Plug the drain, fill the pan to just under the curb, mark the water line and leave it for a day. Check the line has not dropped and look under the floor for any drip. Then open the drain, check it flows freely and leaks nowhere at the nut or the trap.
  9. Seal the plywood before the liner goes on. Two coats of thin epoxy on every cut edge and on the bare plywood of the nook and the valve and head cut-outs; the face under the liner needs only the adhesive, but the edges must be epoxied (water gets in from the edges).
  10. Cut the liner (drawing E): the wall panel (35 1/4 x 69 15/16, with the nook cut out), W3's face (24 x 69 15/16) and the four nook sides, each 1/8 in short at a free edge and 1/4 in above the pan. Hole-saw the head and the valve holes.
  11. Glue the wall panel with the FRP adhesive, rolled on the plywood and pressed on from the middle out with a hand roller; keep it 1/4 in above the pan lip and 1/8 in off W3, the rear edge and the ceiling. Then the nook (back, then sides), then W3's face, then clip the inside corner moulding over the corner with sealant behind each flange (drawing F).
  12. Seal and trim. Fill the 1/4 in gap at the pan with flexible sealant and tool it; seal the rear edge under its edge trim, the top under the trim strip, every nook corner, and behind the head and valve flanges; wipe the excess off straight away. Let it cure a day.
  13. Finish and test. Fit the head and valve, hang the curtain and the nook cord, then run the shower for ten minutes with the curtain closed and check under the floor and along the bottom of W3 for any wet.
