Step 4 of 18
Wiring rough-in

Everything that will be hidden gets wired before it is covered.

12 V DC runs for the fan (up to 2.7 A), the air conditioner (up to 58 A), the 2 ceiling lights and the battery cable; the hot water heater needs about 133 A through the inverter. Decide the battery (280 Ah), inverter and fuses first, since the electrics are still undecided.

The drawings, the wire sizes and the cable table are on the electrical layout page.

Steps, in order

  1. Before the walls and ceiling are covered: run the risers, the ceiling trunk and every branch, with grommets in all steel and the 12 V and 120 V in separate looms (at least 2 in apart, crossing at right angles).
  2. Ceiling first (it is hidden first): the two ceiling lights (16 AWG, each on one slat), the soft LED strip (14 AWG, its own 10 A circuit), the roof fan (14 AWG, 5 A) and the A/C feed (a 6 AWG pair, a 100 A fuse at the battery, about 12 ft).
  3. The 12 V trunk to the fuse block (12 AWG), the water pump circuit (14 AWG, 10 A, under the sink) and the control panel (18 AWG, 3 A).
  4. The 120 V circuits in flexible conduit (12 AWG): the outlets (A), the microwave through the selector (B), the fridge (C) and the water heater through the selector (D). One shore inlet (30 A) in the middle of the cover plate CP (9 in from the back and 13 3/4 in up, the plate is cut for it); its 10 AWG cable runs inside the wall above S2 to the hub board at the closet.
  5. With the furring strips: fix the electrical boxes to blocking between the strips, and add blocking behind the hub board, the switch plates and the outlets before the planks go on. The dinette outlet goes at 34 in up, under the side window frame (not 36).
  6. Last, after the covering is up: the hub board (on the closet's rear panel, 72 to 74 in from the back, 1 to 11 in up) with its fuses, the inverter and the two chargers on the gear shelf, the transfer switch, the lights and the plates. Every cable to the gear shelf needs a loop about 24 in longer than a fixed run.
  7. Test every circuit with a meter before you connect the battery, test the GFCI outlets with their button, and have the 120 V side inspected before you use it.
