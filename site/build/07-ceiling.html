Steps, in order

  1. Blocking first (CB1, CB2, CB3): 21 pieces of pine 2x2 (1 1/2 x 1 1/2 in) in three rails along the van, listed under the drawing. Cut each piece to fit between two roof ribs, 1/16 in short. Bevel the bottom face of each to the roof's tilt there, 16 degrees on CB1 and CB2 and 9 on CB3, the low edge toward the nearer wall (a spare piece laid across two ribs shows the angle). Glue each in with polyurethane construction adhesive against the rib sides and set it flush with the rib bottoms (a straightedge across two ribs shows flush); the ribs hang 2 in and the blocking is 1 1/2, so it stops 1/2 in short of the roof. CB1 is 3 to 4 1/2 in across from the driver wall, CB2 is 63 1/2 to 65, CB3 is 50 1/4 to 51 3/4. Leave the gap from 76 to 86 in from the rear doors empty: the cables across the van pass there. The plywood screws then hold each piece up.
  2. Plywood next: 3 sheets of 3/16 in plywood against the roof, cut around the fan and the A/C openings (their frames are already in from the roof step). Cut six pieces: 59 3/4, 68 and 32 1/4 long, each 48 wide, and the same three 14 wide, so the seams across the van land on the middle of rib 3 and rib 5 (59 3/4 and 127 3/4 in from the rear doors) and the long seam (51 in across) is over CB3; no seam crosses a light hole or an opening. Screw the plywood up into the ribs (self-drilling screws, the ribs are steel) and into the blocking about every 8 in, and leave a 1/16 in gap at each seam.
  3. Lights: the four round lights are Acegoo 12 V slim pucks (2 3/4 in across, 1/2 in thick, held by two spring clips). Each goes in a 2 1/4 in hole through the plywood, centered on a 3 in slat and 2 in clear of the ribs: the shower light and the countertop and sink light on the slat at 58 in across, and two in the center row on the slat at 34 in across (35 7/8 and 102 1/8 from the rear doors). The clips only reach behind thin plywood, not the slat and plywood together (15/16 in), so the slat is cut away 3 in at each light and the puck clips into the plywood alone; its face then sits about 5/8 in behind the slat faces. Cut the hole, pull the wire through it, then clip the puck in. The shower light is wet rated; all four are on plain on/off switches, not dimmers: the shower light on the rear doors plate, the countertop and sink light and the two center-row lights (which share one rocker) on the plate under the TV.
  4. Slats: 15 cedar slats, 3 x 3/4 in with 1 in gaps (1 1/2 in at each side), glued to the plywood; each needs 14 ft stock, or shorter slats in the plan's "for now" option. The two slats with lights (at 58 and 34 in across) each have 3 in gaps cut at the lights.
  5. Cut the slats at the fan and A/C openings; each ends at a framed curb. Cut the 3 in gaps at the four lights too, centered on the light holes.
  6. The soft LED strip goes around the ceiling edge on its own circuit.

4. The ceiling covering, seen from above (diagram G)

The ceiling is covered with 3/16 in plywood against the roof and 3/4 in thick, 3 in wide cedar slats on it, so the finished ceiling is 15/16 in lower, at 75 1/16 in (the bare ceiling is 76 in). Everything that reached the ceiling stops at the new one: the wall cabinets are 14 1/16 in tall (61 to 75 1/16) and the closet and the wet bath are 75 1/16 in. The covering is the 62 in flat part of the roof, centered in the van (3 in each side of the 68 in inside width), so there are 15 slats. They run the whole length of the ceiling (160 in) for now, except where the fan and air conditioning openings cut them and at the four lights (each slat is cut away 3 in there, so the 2 3/4 in puck clips into the plywood alone). The ceiling blocking (the dashed brown strips, CB1 to CB3) is 1 1/2 in wood between the ribs, above the plywood: two rails under the trim along the wall tops and one under the long plywood seam; the plywood seams across the van are on the middle of rib 3 and rib 5, where the steel rib is under them. The covering weighs about 109 lb (37 lb of plywood and 72 lb of cedar), all overhead. The five dashed red bands are the roof ribs, 2 3/4 in wide, drawn at the positions you measured (their edge nearest the rear doors: 21 3/4, 37 7/8, 58 3/8, 97 3/8 and 126 3/8 in); both openings sit between ribs, and the dashed blue squares are the fan unit (17 3/4 square) and the air conditioner unit (28 1/4 square) on the roof. Fan and air conditioner are both in the middle of the van, 34 in across. The wall diagrams B and E show the covering, the lights and the cutouts (their positions are 6 in less than the numbers here, because they are measured from the datum 6 in in from the rear edge), and the interactive page has a Ceiling view with the sizes.

How the pieces go up

  1. Cut the six pieces from three 4 x 8 ft sheets. Sheet 1: PW1 (59 3/4 x 48) and PW5 (32 1/4 x 48), 92 in with the saw cut. Sheet 2: PW3 (68 x 48). Sheet 3: ripped into 14 in strips, one strip for PW2 and PW6 (59 3/4 and 32 1/4) and one for PW4 (68). Number each piece on its back.
  2. Cut the openings and the light holes on the floor, from the table: PW1 has the fan and A/C openings, PW1 to PW4 each have one light hole. Check the two openings against the curbs you built (14 x 14 and 14 1/4 square inside).
  3. Draw the screw lines on the room face of each piece, because the ribs and rails will be hidden once it is up. Rib middles are at 23 1/8, 39 1/4, 59 3/4, 98 3/4 and 127 3/4 in from the rear doors; the rails are at 3 3/4, 51 and 64 1/4 across from the driver wall. At the two seams across the van each piece takes its own half of the rib: put its screw line 3/4 in in from the edge.
  4. Wool first, one bay at a time: fill the bays under a piece with the wool just before that piece goes up (the wool goes in after the blocking, and before the plywood).
  5. Lift PW1 first (rear, driver side: the biggest, with the openings), with a helper and two props (2x4s cut to the ceiling height) in the middle. Push it flat against the ribs and rails, then start screwing at the middle rib and work outward, every 8 in: self-drilling screws with washer heads into the steel ribs, 1 in wood screws with washers into the rails.
  6. PW2 next (the 14 in strip beside it): it butts PW1 over CB3 with a 1/16 in gap; each piece takes half of CB3. Then PW3 and PW4 the same way, butting PW1 and PW2 on the middle of rib 3; then PW5 and PW6 on the middle of rib 5.
  7. Leave the 1/16 in gaps at every seam. Do not fill them: the slats, glued across, hold the seams flat.
