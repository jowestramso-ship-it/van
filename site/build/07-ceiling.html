Steps, in order

  1. Furring strips first (CB1 to CB6): six strips, 1/2 in thick and 2 1/2 in wide, running the whole 160 in of the ceiling lengthwise, screwed to the bottoms of the steel ribs; listed under the drawing. Each strip is three pieces, 23 1/8, 75 5/8 and 61 1/4 in long, joined on the middle of rib 1 and rib 4 (never at the plywood seams, which are on rib 3 and rib 5), so twelve 8 ft sticks in all (one 4 x 8 ft sheet of 1/2 in plywood ripped to 2 1/2 in gives 19). They run across the van, centered, at 4 1/4, 14, 24 1/4, 43 5/8, 51 and 63 3/4 in from the driver wall: CB1 and CB2 under the wall-top trim, their outer edges on the plywood edge, CB3 under the long plywood seam, CB5 and CB6 on each side of the fan and A/C openings, CB4 between CB1 and CB5. Lay each strip flat against the rib bottoms (it tilts with the roof near the walls), pull it straight with a string line, and screw it to every rib it crosses with two self-drilling screws about 1 in apart; at a joint each piece takes its own half of the rib, its screws 3/4 in from the rib's middle. The strips lower the finished ceiling by 1/2 in: the cables run in the 2 in bays above them, so nothing needs a gap, and the wool goes in after the strips and before the plywood.
  2. Plywood next: 3 sheets of 3/16 in plywood on the strips, cut around the fan and the A/C openings (their frames are already in from the roof step). Cut six pieces: 59 3/4, 68 and 32 1/4 long, each 48 wide, and the same three 14 wide, with the seams across the van at 59 3/4 and 127 3/4 in from the rear doors and the long seam (51 in across) over CB3; no seam crosses a light hole or an opening. Screw the plywood into the strips about every 8 in with 5/8 in screws with washers (they go through the 3/16 in plywood and 7/16 in into the strip), and leave a 1/16 in gap at each seam. Diagram H shows the six pieces and the order they go up.
  3. Lights: the four round lights are Acegoo 12 V slim pucks (2 3/4 in across, 1/2 in thick, held by two spring clips). Each goes in a 2 1/4 in hole through the plywood, centered on a 3 in slat and 2 in clear of the ribs: the shower light and the countertop and sink light on the slat at 58 in across, and two in the center row on the slat at 34 in across (35 7/8 and 102 1/8 from the rear doors). The clips only reach behind thin plywood, not the slat and plywood together (15/16 in), so the slat is cut away 3 in at each light and the puck clips into the plywood alone; its face then sits about 5/8 in behind the slat faces. Cut the hole, pull the wire through it, then clip the puck in. The shower light is wet rated; all four are on plain on/off switches, not dimmers: the shower light on the rear doors plate, the countertop and sink light and the two center-row lights (which share one rocker) on the plate under the TV.
  4. Slats: 15 cedar slats, 3 x 3/4 in with 1 in gaps (1 1/2 in at each side), glued to the plywood; each needs 14 ft stock, or shorter slats in the plan's "for now" option. The two slats with lights (at 58 and 34 in across) each have 3 in gaps cut at the lights.
  5. Cut the slats at the fan and A/C openings; each ends at a framed curb. The strips add 1/2 in under the ribs, so the finished ceiling is now 1/2 in lower than the curbs were built for: the fan curb (2 15/16 tall) stops 1/2 in short of the slat faces, so glue a ring of the same 1/2 in material, 3/4 in wide, under each curb; check the A/C curb against the new finished plane (1 7/16 below the rib bottoms) before it is mounted. Cut the 3 in gaps at the four lights too, centered on the light holes.
  6. The soft LED strip goes around the ceiling edge on its own circuit.

4. The ceiling covering, seen from above (diagram G)

The ceiling is built from six lengthwise furring strips, 1/2 in thick, screwed to the bottoms of the steel ribs, then 3/16 in plywood screwed to the strips, then 3/4 in thick, 3 in wide cedar slats glued on the plywood: so the finished ceiling is 1 7/16 in below the bottoms of the ribs, at 74 1/16 in (the ribs are 75 1/2 in up at the wall). Everything that reaches the ceiling stops at the new one: the closet and the wet bath are 74 1/16 in tall and the overhead cabinets top out there. The covering is the 62 in flat part of the roof, centered in the van (3 in each side of the 68 in inside width), so there are 15 slats. They run the whole length of the ceiling (160 in) for now, except where the fan and air conditioning openings cut them and at the four lights (each slat is cut away 3 in there, so the 2 3/4 in puck clips into the plywood alone). The six furring strips (the dashed brown lines, CB1 to CB6) run lengthwise under the ribs, above the plywood: CB1 and CB2 under the trim along the wall tops, CB3 under the long plywood seam, CB5 and CB6 on each side of the fan and air conditioning openings and CB4 between CB1 and CB5; each is three pieces joined on the middle of rib 1 and rib 4. The covering weighs about 133 lb (37 lb of plywood, 72 lb of cedar and about 24 lb of strips), all overhead. The five dashed red bands are the roof ribs, 2 3/4 in wide, drawn at the positions you measured (their edge nearest the rear doors: 21 3/4, 37 7/8, 58 3/8, 97 3/8 and 126 3/8 in); both openings sit between ribs, and the dashed blue squares are the fan unit (17 3/4 square) and the air conditioner unit (28 1/4 square) on the roof. Fan and air conditioner are both in the middle of the van, 34 in across. The wall diagrams B and E show the covering, the lights and the cutouts (their positions are 6 in less than the numbers here, because they are measured from the datum 6 in in from the rear edge), and the interactive page has a Ceiling view with the sizes.

The piece names are the strip and the number: CB1.1 to CB1.3, CB2.1 to CB2.3, and so on. Cut them 1/16 in short at each joint.

Twelve 8 ft sticks make the six strips: one stick for the 23 1/8 and the 61 1/4 pieces (84 3/8 in with the saw cut) and one for the 75 5/8 piece, for each strip. Each strip lies flat on the rib bottoms and tilts with the roof: near the walls the roof is an arc, up to 16 degrees across the strip, so do not try to keep it level: lay it against the rib bottoms, screw it there, and check it with a string line along its length before the plywood goes up.

How the pieces go up

  1. Cut the six pieces from three 4 x 8 ft sheets. Sheet 1: PW1 (59 3/4 x 48) and PW5 (32 1/4 x 48), 92 in with the saw cut. Sheet 2: PW3 (68 x 48). Sheet 3: ripped into 14 in strips, one strip for PW2 and PW6 (59 3/4 and 32 1/4) and one for PW4 (68). Number each piece on its back.
  2. Cut the openings and the light holes on the floor, from the table: PW1 has the fan and A/C openings, PW1 to PW4 each have one light hole. Check the two openings against the curbs you built (14 x 14 and 14 1/4 square inside).
  3. Draw the screw lines on the room face of each piece, because the strips will be hidden once it is up. The six strips, 2 1/2 in wide, run lengthwise at 4 1/4, 14, 24 1/4, 43 5/8, 51 and 63 3/4 in across from the driver wall (their centers). At CB3, under the long seam, each piece takes its own half of the strip: its screw line is 3/8 in from the seam.
  4. Wool first, one bay at a time: fill the bays under a piece with the wool just before that piece goes up (the wool goes in after the strips are on, and before the plywood; the space between the roof and the plywood is now 2 1/2 in: the 2 in of the ribs and the 1/2 in strips).
  5. Lift PW1 first (rear, driver side: the biggest, with the openings), with a helper and two props (2x4s cut to the ceiling height) in the middle. Push it flat against the strips, then start screwing at the middle and work outward, every 8 in along each strip: 5/8 in screws with washers (they go through the 3/16 in plywood and 7/16 in into the 1/2 in strip).
  6. PW2 next (the 14 in strip beside it): it butts PW1 over CB3 with a 1/16 in gap; each piece takes half of CB3. Then PW3 and PW4 the same way, butting PW1 and PW2 at 59 3/4 in; then PW5 and PW6 at 127 3/4 in. The strips run under every seam, so the plywood screws into a strip all along each edge.
  7. Leave the 1/16 in gaps at every seam. Do not fill them: the slats, glued across, hold the seams flat.
