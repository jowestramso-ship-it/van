Steps, in order

  1. The steps below are a suggested order made from the plan's own sizes. The plan has no drawn, step-by-step instructions for this part yet, so check each one against the real parts before you cut.
  2. Frame the shower nook between the nook frames V1 and V2 (the furring page), centered on the 36 in stall (9 3/8 to 26 5/8 in from the back). There is no end wall: the cab end of the stall, next to the fridge, is a curtain.
  3. The pan (36 x 24 in, 3 in curb, bought, fixed) is set, drained and tested, and the back wall lined with PVC paneling, on the previous page, before this one.
  4. Plumb it: the shower head at 7 in from the back, 70 up, left of the nook, and the valve at 11 in, 36 up, under the rear end of the nook (holes through the paneling, sealed behind their flanges); the hot and cold lines rise at 9 3/4 in, left of the first block of S4, and the feed to the head rises at 7 in; the drain runs from the rear-door end of the pan to a tank under the van (the grey tank's place is not set).
  5. Curtains on three sides: bend the ceiling rail to a U, 1 1/2 in inside the pan's edge (33 in across the front and 22 1/2 in down each end), fix it up into the ceiling slats, and hang the two curtains from it so they overlap 6 in in the middle of the front, with small weights in the hems, inside the curb (diagram E3). Pull them aside to the two ends when you are not showering.
  6. Toilet: the Reliance Fold-To-Go stays set up on the pan at the cab end (19 1/2 to 34 in, its back half an inch short of the cab-end curtain), sideways.
  7. Nook contents: towel bar, a shelf, toilet paper and bottles (keep the paper out of the splash). The nook has no front lip, so water runs out over the open edge: screw an eye into each end of the nook and stretch a bungee cord across the front, about 5 1/2 in above the shelf board, to keep the bottles from falling out.

Drawings and numbers

E3. Shower curtains on three sides, with a fixed pan and the toilet at the cab end

Two flexible plastic curtains, like a home shower curtain, hang free from one rail at the ceiling on the three open sides of the stall (the back wall, along the passenger wall, is lined with PVC paneling, glued over the plywood), and slide round on it (left: looking down at the 36 x 24 in pan). The rod runs 1 1/2 in inside the pan's edge, 33 in across the front and 22 1/2 in down each end, and the two curtains overlap 6 in in the middle of the front. The only rail is the one at the ceiling (a bendable ceiling track bent to a U, fixed up into the ceiling slats; it replaces the wall W3 the plan had at the cab end); there is no rod in the hem: the bottom is free, with small weights (a light stainless chain or sewn-in weights) that keep it inside the 3 in curb in use. Right: from the rear, along the wall.

The toilet. The Reliance Fold-To-Go (14 1/2 in square and 14 1/2 in high, a folding seat on four legs that holds a bag) stays set up on the pan at the cab end, sideways, with its back half an inch short of the cab-end curtain, next to the fridge (19 1/2 to 34 in from the back; there is no wall between the shower and the fridge now, so the fridge's side stands just outside the curtain). That leaves 19 1/2 in of the pan in front of it to stand and shower, and 4 3/4 in each side of it. It gets wet when you shower: wipe the seat. The drain is at the middle of the rear-door end of the pan (5 in from the rear end, as you said, and 12 out from the wall, the middle of the depth: assumed, check it on the pan), the pan sloping to it, and runs straight down through the floor to the grey water tank under the van. The pan is bought (36 x 24), fixed and does not tip; the Shower pan page of the build guide shows how to set it.

Things to watch: (1) no stiff rod in the hem, so the hem stays free and the weights hold it inside the 3 in curb; (2) the rod runs to the wall so the curtain seals the ends; (3) wipe the pan and the curtains dry, or the plastic mildews; (4) the toilet paper in the shower nook is about 12 in along the wall from the shower head and faces the stall, so give it a small hinged door or a lidded holder; (5) hold the curtains aside with a loop or a magnet at the ceiling while driving. Total fabric: about 80 to 100 in wide by 74 in long, as two curtains (each about 40 to 50 in wide), cut from standard 72 in curtains.
