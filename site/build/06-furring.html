Furring rails, spacer blocks and shims

Eight furring rails on each wall, so every plank is held at least every 14 in.

The eight horizontal furring rails on each wall (1x4 boards laid flat, 3/4 x 3 1/2 in, centered at 3, 12, 21 1/4, 30 1/4, 43 1/2, 57 1/4, 66, 76 3/4 in up, at most 13 3/4 in apart center to center; each sits on spacer blocks 3 5/8 out (lying on their side, 3 1/2 along the wall x 1 1/2 up, on S4 and S6; standing up, 1 1/2 along x 3 1/2 up, on S5 and the frames), or on the steel; S7 stops at the sliding door opening and only S8 carries over it), On the driver side S5 and S6 stop at a four-piece frame around the side window, and the dinette outlet sits at 34 in up under it; behind the couch S5 is cut away between two 1x2 frames, V5 at 3 1/2 to 5 in and V6 at 74 to 75 1/2 in, each on three blocks, with S4 as the nook's bottom edge and S6 as its top edge (the opening is 32 to 55 1/2 in up), then the nook is lined with 1/4 in plywood (a back glued to the ribs, a floor on S4, a ceiling under S6, two sides); then the blocking for the ceiling slats. Do the ceiling blocking before the walls.

Steps, in order

  1. Cut the furring rails to the cut list below: the eight main furring rails S1 to S8 are 1x4 boards laid flat (3/4 x 3 1/2 in), 34 pieces from about 23 eight-foot sticks; the window frame and the nook frames and cross pieces are 1x2 (3/4 x 1 1/2 in), 13 pieces from about 4 sticks.   4. Glue the blocks to the steel (adhesive, never screws into the van's body) at the dots of S4, S5 and S6, the side window frame J1 to J4, the nook frames V1 to V3 and the couch nook frames V5 and V6, with adhesive pads for S1, S2 and S3, and hold them until cured.
  5. Fix the furring rails to the blocks: glue and a #8 x 1 1/2 in screw through the furring rail into each block (the 3 1/2 in furring rail lies on the block's full height). S7 and S8 sit on their shims.
  6.   7. Behind the couch (driver side): the couch is 69 in long, 5 to 74 in, and the wall behind it has a nook 5 to 74 in along and 32 to 55 1/2 in up, over the pans of the steel, for the back cushion to tilt into. Leave S5 out from the back to 75 1/2 in, and put in two 1x2 frames, V5 (3 1/2 to 5 in along) and V6 (74 to 75 1/2 in), each 23 1/2 in long between S4 and S6 and on three blocks. What "laid flat" means

The eight main furring rails are 1x4 boards. A 1x4 measures 3/4 in thick and 3 1/2 in wide. Laid flat means the 3 1/2 in wide face looks into the room, so the board sticks out only 3/4 in from the spacers behind it. The drawing shows the board cut across, the same board laid flat (how it is used), on edge (not used), and as you see it from inside the room.

How to attach a block to the steel

All 71 blocks go on the same way, with adhesive or tape and never with screws. The drawing shows one block in section; the steps follow.

  1. Mark the dots. From the chart above, mark each block's place on the steel with a pencil or a bit of tape (its distance from the back mark and its height up). Each one goes on solid steel at the dot, clear of the holes and of the edges of the pans. On S4 and S6 the block lies on the 1 1/2 in strip of steel (29 1/2 to 31 up, and 56 1/2 to 58 up), so mark the strip's two edges, not the block's height.
  2. Prepare the steel at each mark, a little bigger than the block: remove loose paint and rust; wipe off any oil or wax; scuff lightly with a gray Scotch-Brite pad or 400 grit paper until the paint is evenly dull (if you go through to bare steel, touch it with a rust-inhibiting primer first); vacuum the dust; wipe with a 70/30 mix of isopropyl alcohol and water on a lint-free cloth and dry it with another; do not touch it again.
  3. Prepare the block. Sand the face smooth and wipe off the dust. For VHB tape, also seal the face with a coat of polyurethane and let it dry, because raw wood is porous.
  4. Put the adhesive on the block, not on the steel. Polyurethane adhesive (Sikaflex-252 or similar): a bead round the edge of the 1 1/2 x 3 1/2 in face and one down the middle, enough to leave a 1/8 in bond line when pressed. It works from 50 to 95 F and stays workable for only about 35 minutes at 73 F, so press each block on within that time. VHB tape (about 110 mil, so the 3 1/2 in block plus the tape is the 3 5/8 in standoff): put the tape on the block face first, press it firmly, then peel the liner. Apply it only when the steel, the block and the tape are between 70 and 100 F, never below about 50 F, and with no condensation on the steel.
  5. Set the block on its mark, square to the wall: lying on its side (3 1/2 along the wall, 1 1/2 up) on S4 and S6, standing up (1 1/2 along, 3 1/2 up) on S5 and under the frames, and press it on at once (no sliding it around). Use a furring rail as the gauge: lay one of the furring rail's own pieces across the faces of the blocks of that furring rail, with wax paper or plastic wrap between so it does not stick, and press each block against it, so they all end on one line. With VHB press hard, about 15 psi, which on this face is about 79 lb, for a minute, and brace it. With adhesive hold it with masking tape or a prop until it sets. Nothing is screwed into the van body: there are no holes through to the outside.
  6. Leave it a day or more before it takes any load. Then lay a straightedge across the blocks of one furring rail: their outer faces should be within about 1/16 in of each other (sand a high block, or shim a low one with thin veneer), so the furring rail's back sits 3 3/4 in off the body along its whole length.
  7. Then the furring rail: wood glue on the block, the furring rail on top, and one #8 x 1 1/2 in screw through the furring rail into the block, in a pilot hole. S1, S2 and S3 (adhesive pads) and S7 and S8 (shims) get their pad or shim glued to the steel the same way (steps 2 to 6), and the furring rail goes on top of it.
  8. Test one block first. Glue one block to a spot that will be hidden and try to pry it off with a flat bar after a day: it should tear the wood or the paint off the steel, not let go clean. If it lets go clean, the steel was not clean enough or the wrong adhesive for it.

The couch nook: the plywood lining and the sides

The nook behind the couch is lined with 1/4 in plywood, all cut from one sheet: a back glued to the ribs of the steel, a floor on top of S4, a ceiling under S6, and a side at each end against the frames V5 and V6. The lining keeps the cushion off the bare steel and closes the gap behind the rails (S4 and S6 stand 3 3/4 in off the body, so without a floor and a ceiling the nook would open into the wall cavity). It leaves the nook 4.5 in deep from the face of the walls to the back, which is the number the cushion's tilt was worked out for.

How to build it, in order:

  1. Cut the five pieces from one 4 x 8 sheet of 1/4 in plywood (the sizes are in the table): the back A 69 x 23 3/8 in, the floor B and the ceiling C 69 x 4 1/2 in each, and the two sides D 23 x 4 1/2 in each. Cut the 6 x 4 3/4 in notch in A's lower rear corner: mark it from the real steel, because the angle there stands 1 1/2 in off the body (up to 36 3/4 in at 3 to 6 in along, sloping down to 31 up at 11 in), and a flat sheet cannot lie on it.
  2. Finish every piece before it goes in: sand, and seal and finish (or paint) both faces and all edges. The nook is hard to reach once it is built, and the plywood walls are not on yet.
  3. Mark the ribs on the steel. The lining sits on the ribs, the 3 in strips between the pans that stand 1/8 in off the body: from the back mark they are at 5 to 6, 16 to 19 1/2, 29 3/4 to 32 3/4, 43 to 46, 56 1/4 to 61 and 69 5/8 to 73. The pans between them are 1/8 in lower and the lining bridges them. Scuff and clean the ribs as for the blocks. Do this when S4, S6, V5 and V6 are on and S5 is out of the way, before the plywood walls.
  4. Fit the back dry. Slide A in from the front between S4 and S6 and press it on the ribs: it should lie flat on all of them, with the notch clear of the angle. Trim the notch to the steel.
  5. Glue the back to the ribs. Put two beads of adhesive down each rib (not on the pans), press A on, and hold it with painter's tape to S4 and S6 and a few light props for a day. Nothing is screwed into the van's body.
  6. The floor. Lay B on top of S4 with its back edge against the face of A, glue it to A and to S4, and screw it down into S4 every 8 in, 3/4 in from the front edge (pilot holes, countersunk). Its front edge ends flush with the face of the walls.
  7. The ceiling. The same under S6: glue C to A and to S6's underside and screw it up into S6 every 8 in.
  8. The sides. Fit each D between the floor and the ceiling at the end of the nook, against the inside face of V5 (at 5 in) or V6 (at 74 in). Glue its edges to A, B and C and its back face to the frame, and nail it with 1 in brads every 4 in, through D and into the frame, which is 3/4 in thick. Wipe the glue off at once.
  9. The covering. Cut the opening in the 3/8 in plywood sheets at 5 to 74 in along and 32 to 55 1/2 in up, so the edges of the walls meet the outside faces of D, B and C, and finish the 1/4 in front edges of B, C and D with iron-on veneer tape or a thin nosing (the Walls page has the edge details).
  10. Try the cushion: with the back cushion tilted 12 degrees, its base stands 1 13/16 in out from the wall and its top edge goes about 2 1/2 in into the nook, leaving about 2 in behind it.

Also cut: spacer blocks (not furring rails), and what holds them

The wood blocks are the spacers that hold S4, S5, S6, the window frame (J1 to J4), the shower and counter nook frames V1 to V3 and the couch nook frames V5 and V6 off the steel so every furring rail's back sits on the same line, 3 3/4 in off the body. Each is a 3 1/2 in cut of 2x4 that is 3 1/2 in out from the steel. On S4 and S6 it lies on its side: 3 1/2 along the wall, 1 1/2 up, exactly the height of the 1 1/2 in strip of steel it sits on (the steel stands 1 1/2 in off the body below S4's strip and 3 in off above S6's, so a block standing up would run into it). On S5 and under the frames it stands up: 1 1/2 along the wall, 3 1/2 up, on the ribs, where the steel is flat above and below.

  • Size and stock: cut 3 1/2 in pieces off a 2x4 (a 2x4 is really 1 1/2 x 3 1/2 in) so each block is 1 1/2 x 3 1/2 in on the steel and 3 1/2 in out (lying on its side on S4 and S6, standing up on S5 and the frames). The 1/8 in glue line under it makes the 3 5/8 in standoff, and the grain runs along the 3 1/2 in cut, which is never the direction out from the wall, so the furring rail's screw goes into side grain (a block with its grain pointing at the furring rail would take that screw in end grain, which holds about half as well). On S4 and S6 the block lies on the 1 1/2 in strip of steel (29 1/2 to 31 and 56 1/2 to 58 up): the steel is 1/8 in off the body there, so the 3 5/8 in standoff puts the furring rail's back on the common line.
  • Block to the steel: adhesive, not screws. Nothing is drilled into the van's body. Use a polyurethane construction adhesive (the kind van builders use is Sikaflex 252 or a similar one; check the label for steel and for a bond line of about 1/8 in) or 3M VHB tape. Put it only on solid steel, away from the holes, and test one block on a scrap of your own steel (pull it off after a day) before you do the wall.
  • If you use polyurethane adhesive instead of tape (Sikaflex-252 is one): Sika's data sheet gives an application range of 50 to 95 F (10 to 35 C), best at 59 to 77 F (15 to 25 C), a skin time of about 40 minutes and an open time of about 35 minutes at 73 F, so press each block on within half an hour and hold it (masking tape or a prop) until it has set; leave it a day or more before loading it. Painted steel may need Sika's primer or activator (see its pre-treatment chart, and test one block). The prep is the same as below: scuff, wipe off the dust, 70/30 isopropyl alcohol and water, dry. Do not use screws or rivnuts into the steel: at the dots it is the van's outer skin or a rib pressed from it, so a screw would go through the outside of the van.
  • If you use VHB tape, temperature: apply it when the steel, the block and the tape are all between 70 and 100 F (21 to 38 C); do not apply it below about 50 F (10 C), where the adhesive is too stiff to wet the surface (some grades say 60 F). Warm the steel with the van heater or a heat lamp if you have to, keep it out of direct sun that heats the steel well past 100 F, and make sure there is no condensation on it. Once the tape is on, it holds in the cold.
  • If you use VHB tape, steel prep: (1) remove loose paint, rust and dust, and make sure the factory paint under the tape is sound, because the tape is only as good as the paint it sticks to; (2) if the steel is oily or waxy, wipe it first with a degreaser or solvent; (3) scuff lightly to dull the gloss, with a gray (ultra fine) or maroon (very fine) Scotch-Brite pad or 400 to 420 grit sandpaper, only until the paint looks evenly dull and not through to bare steel (if you do hit bare steel, touch it up with a rust-inhibiting primer before taping), then wipe the dust off; (4) wipe with a 70/30 mix of isopropyl alcohol and water on a clean lint-free cloth, then wipe dry with another; (5) do not touch the cleaned spot. Seal the face of each wood block (a coat of polyurethane or similar, dry) or sand it smooth first, because raw wood is porous.
  • If you use VHB tape, applying it: put the tape on the block first, press it firmly, peel the liner, set the block in place (no sliding it around), then press hard, about 15 psi, which on a 1 1/2 x 3 1/2 in block (5 1/4 sq in) is about 79 lb, for a minute, and hold or brace it. The bond keeps building for a day or more, so leave the furring rails off it until the next day. Choose a foam thickness near 1/8 in (110 mil) so the 3 1/2 in block plus the tape is the 3 5/8 in standoff. Read the data sheet of the exact tape you buy: the numbers above are the ones 3M gives for its VHB tapes in general.
  • Furring rail to the block: wood glue or construction adhesive plus one #8 x 1 1/2 in screw through the furring rail into the block.
  • How many: 42 on the driver side (S4 12, S5 3, S6 9, 12 under the window frame, 3 under each of J1 to J4, and 6 under the couch nook frames V5 and V6) and 29 on the passenger side (S4 8, S5 4 in the middle piece only, S6 8, and 3 each under V1, V2 and V3), so 71.
