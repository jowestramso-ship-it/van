Steps, in order

  1. Seal and finish both faces of every sheet first (a clear water-based finish or hardwax oil), before you hang it.
  2. Hang the sheets floor to ceiling, glued to every furring rail, with a screw and a finish washer about every 8 in and 1/16 in between sheets; the joints fall at 42, 90 and 138 in.
  3. Stand the three 3/4 in floor-to-ceiling walls (W1 closet end, W2 behind the driver seat, W4 counter side of the fridge) on the finished floor and fix them to the framing; the dinette backs lean on W1 and W2. The shower has no end wall: its three open sides are curtains (the Wet bath page).
  4. Then cover the corners and edges (the table below): after the sheets are up and before the cabinets, so the cabinets and the trim meet cleanly.

Covering the corners and the edges of 3/8 in plywood

The 3/8 in plywood shows its layers on every cut edge, and the van flexes and the wood moves with the weather, so the rule is: hide the edges behind wood trim or banding, leave a 1/16 to 1/8 in gap that the trim covers, and never fill a corner with hard filler or tight caulk that will crack. Fix the trim with wood glue and 1 in (18 gauge) brads or #6 x 1 in finish-washer screws; the 3/8 in sheet itself is glued to every furring rail and screwed (#6 x 1 in) with a finish washer about every 8 in. Finish every trim piece on all faces before it goes on (the same clear water-based finish or hardwax oil as the sheets).

The wall sheet is now 3/8 in thick (it was 1/8), so each sheet weighs about three times as much (about 180 lb for both walls instead of about 60) and the finished wall stands 1/4 in further into the van, so the width between the finished walls is 1/2 in less unless the furring rail line is pulled back 1/4 in. Check the length of every screw: #6 x 1 in goes through the 3/8 in sheet and 5/8 in into the 3/4 in furring rail.
