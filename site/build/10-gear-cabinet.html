The gear cabinet and its sliding shelf

Floor-mounted, after the battery is in; the couch frame rests on it afterwards.

You asked for a cabinet for the battery and the three units, fixed to the floor, with the top part sliding out. It is two plywood side panels on the floor and the gear shelf as the sliding top; the front below the shelf stays open.

C4. Driver side: the gear cabinet and its sliding shelf, how to make it

The inverter, the DC-DC charger and the shore charger all sit on one tray over the battery. The cabinet is two 3/4 in plywood side panels standing on the floor, screwed down through floor cleats, as deep as the couch (26 in) and as tall as the space under the deck (16 in). It has no front, back or top of its own: the shelf is its sliding top, and the battery stays on the floor between the panels, in the open. The tray is 1/2 in plywood between two 3/4 x 2 1/2 in end rails, on two 18 in full-extension slides, one on each panel. Its plain face is flush with the cabinet front, so you pull the face and the whole shelf comes out 18 in into the aisle, with every unit and terminal open from above. The battery has a 1 in gap over it for its terminals and lugs. The stack, from the floor: battery 9 1/2, gap 1, tray 1/2, the tallest unit (the DC-DC) 4 1/2, air 1/2, which is the 16 in under the deck; that is why the couch was raised 3 1/2 in. Nothing crosses the front under the deck (the old lift-out rail is dropped): the couch frame (diagram C5) rests on the panels and holds their tops together.

How to build it, in order:

  1. Mark the cabinet on the floor after the subfloor is down and the battery is placed (the battery stays on the floor between the panels, 52 to 73 in from the back, 1 in from the east panel): the west panel at 38 5/8 to 39 3/8 in from the back, the east panel at 74 in, both 26 in out from the wall. Check the panels will be parallel within 1/16 in over their length, because the slides must run parallel.
  2. Cut the panels and the cleats. Two panels 3/4 x 16 x 26, two cleats 3/4 x 1 1/2 x 26, and drill two 3/8 in dowel holes 1/2 in deep in the top edge of each panel (4 in from each end, so the couch frame locates on them). Glue and screw each cleat to its panel, flush with the bottom, on the outer side.
  3. Stand the panels. Set each on its marks, plumb, with the cleat screwed to the floor (#10 x 2 in about every 5 in, or bolted to a floor anchor); then check parallel and square again. Do not fix anything to the wall: the cabinet stands on the floor, and the couch frame (diagram C5) rests on its tops and ties them together.
  4. Mark the slide height with a story stick. The tray bottom sits 10 1/2 in up (1 in over the battery top). Each slide is 1 3/4 in tall and centered on the rail, so its center is 11 3/4 in up and its top 12 5/8. Screw the fixed members to the faces of the two panels (with 5/8 in screws, so they do not come out through the 3/4 in plywood), their front ends flush with the front of the cabinet, both at the same height.
  5. Build the tray. Cut the plywood and the two rails, glue and screw the rails to the ends of the tray with the bottoms flush, glue on the rear lip, and check the diagonals match to 1/16 in. The tray with its rails is 33 5/16 wide, so it leaves 5/8 in each side for a 1/2 in slide and about 1/8 in of clearance.
  6. Screw the moving members to the outer faces of the rails, with the tray upside down on a flat bench, then set the tray on the fixed members and push it home. If the slides bind, shim a panel by 1/16 in or loosen the screws and re-seat the members; do not force them.
  7. Fit the face to the front of the tray and the rails, flush with the cabinet front (25 1/4 to 26 in out from the wall), from 10 1/4 to 14 1/4 in up; nothing covers the front below the face, and nothing crosses it above (the deck is 1 3/4 in over the face), so the battery and the lawn chairs stay in view and in reach.
  8. Set the gear on the tray. Rear row: the DC-DC charger at 41 1/16 to 57 1/16 in along the wall and the inverter at 57 9/16 to 72 3/8; front row: the shore charger at 41 1/16 to 50 9/16; 1/2 in between the units, 1/4 in from the rails and the face. Hold each down through its mounting ears (the 4 1/2 in DC-DC leaves 1/2 in under the deck). Keep the inverter's fan side facing the open front, not a panel.
  9. Wire it with loops. Every cable from the battery and the starter battery, the chargers' AC inputs and the inverter's AC output runs over the rear lip to a U-shaped loop 24 in longer than a fixed run (2/0 and 2 AWG cable bends about 4 in tight, so make the loop 8 in deep or more), tied down along the east panel and the tray rail with clamps so it cannot catch the slide. Fuses stay fixed on the battery side: the Class T within 7 in of the post.
  10. Test it with the couch frame lifted off: pull the face, watch the loops, check the shelf clears the fridge by 3 in, then push it home. About 28 lb loaded: check the sag at full extension (it should be well under 1/8 in with the units on). Then set the couch frame, its deck on top, over the cabinet (diagram C5): the deck must not touch the face.

Not settled: the exact slide model (check its thickness and height against the 1/2 in and 1 3/4 in drawn here), the battery's real terminal height (1 in of gap assumes right-angle lugs), the real weights of the two chargers (28 lb is a guess apart from the inverter's 7.7 lb), where the cables enter the east panel, how the cleats are fixed to the floor (find the floor's anchors or crossmembers first), and the deck's front edge: across the 34 5/8 in between the panels nothing is under it, so load it with a person before the cushions go on. A seated 6 ft head is 3 1/2 in under the wall cabinets (raised to 63 in).
