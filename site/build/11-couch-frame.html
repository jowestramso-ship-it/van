C5. Driver side: the removable couch (frame, wall blocks and deck lid), how to make it

The couch is a separate piece that sits over the gear cabinet (diagram C4) and lifts out in two lifts. The frame has a full-length back rail (74 in, 3/4 x 3 1/2 on edge) that rests on three wall blocks at 3, 20 (if needed) and 41 in from the back mark and is screwed down into them from above; west of the cabinet (0 to 38 5/8 in) a front rail, an end rail and a cross rail make a ladder carried by two posts with screw-out feet, with the lawn chairs between them. Over the cabinet there is no front rail: the deck simply rests on the tops of the two panels, because the units on the shelf are taller than the room under a rail. The deck lid (3/4 in plywood, 74 x 26, with the leaf and cushions of diagrams C, C2 and C3) sits on the frame and on two dowels in each panel top and is screwed to the rails from above. Nothing is fixed to the floor except the cabinet, and nothing is fixed to the cabinet except by its dowels.

How to build it, in order:

  1. Make the three blocks. Cut a 2x4 into 3 in pieces (a stop block on the saw keeps them identical), check they are square, and seal the faces. The block at 20 in is the one you said "maybe, if needed": cut it, but fit it only if the frame feels springy.
  2. Mark them on the wall. Centers at 3, 20 and 41 in from the back mark, all with the bottom at 9 in up (the top at 12 1/2 in up): draw one level line along the wall, because the back rail rests on all three tops. The strip S2 sits at 11 1/4 to 12 3/4 in up behind the planks, so each block overlaps it by 1 1/4 in; put the screws at about 12 in up.
  3. Screw each block on: a bead of construction adhesive on its back, then three #8 x 3 in screws through the block and the 3/8 in planks into S2 (pilot holes). S2 is glued to the steel only at its attachment dots (15, 30 3/4, 40, 54 in): the block at 41 is next to a dot, the one at 20 is 5 in from one, but the block at 3 is 12 in from the nearest, so add an adhesive pad on solid steel behind it when the strips go up (or move the block to a dot).
  4. Build the frame on the bench: the back rail (74 long), the front rail (38 5/8), the end rail and the cross rail (24 1/2), all 3/4 x 3 1/2 on edge, glued and screwed into a ladder, with the tops flush. Check the diagonal of the west rectangle. Glue and screw the two posts to the front rail's inside face, fit the T-nuts and feet, and screw the feet in so each post and foot is about 12 1/2 in to start.
  5. Lift the frame in: the back rail goes onto the three blocks, the posts on the floor, the east end of the back rail resting against the east panel and the back of the cabinet. Adjust the two feet until the tops of the rails are level (use a level on the front rail and a straightedge from the front rail over the west panel top to the east panel top); the panel tops are at 16 in, and so are the rails.
  6. Screw the rail down to each block, two 1/4 x 4 1/2 in structural screws through its top edge (3/16 in pilot holes, 1 in into the block). Do not fix the frame to the cabinet or the floor: it hangs on the wall and stands on its posts.
  7. Set the deck lid on the frame and on the panel dowels and screw it to the rails from above. Pull the gear shelf out and push it home: it must clear the lid by 1 3/4 in at the face. Then put a person on the front edge of the lid over the cabinet (34 5/8 in unsupported between the panels) before the cushions go on; if it sags, a stiff front beam that lifts out is the fix (the rail that was dropped), because anything fixed under the deck there is in the way of the units.
  8. To take the couch out: cushions and leaf off, the lid's screws out and the lid lifted off (about 45 lb, two people), then the six rail screws, and the frame lifts off the blocks (about 40 lb). Everything else, the cabinet, the shelf and the battery, stays.

Not settled: the deck's front edge over the cabinet (34 5/8 in with nothing under it: test it, and keep the old lift-out rail in mind as the fix), whether the block at 3 in has an S2 attachment dot behind it, the weight of the whole couch (about 85 lb with the cushions is an estimate), and how the rail screws find solid wood in the blocks (use a drill stop and pilot holes).
