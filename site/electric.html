Electrical and lighting layout

A proposal for where the electrics go, drawn on the same steel and furring strips as the wall marking map, with the furniture outlined for reference. Positions are inches from the back mark (0 is 6 in in from the rear-door edge) and up from the floor; the ceiling plan uses inches from the rear-door edge. The steel and strips are the measured drawing; the electrical items are my proposal, so check each one against the real wall and tell me what to move.

What you chose: a 2000 W inverter, charging from the alternator and from shore power (no solar), a 120 V fridge, and the water pump under the sink. What I assumed (tell me if wrong):
  • The battery is lithium, 280 Ah at 12.8 V (about 3.6 kWh), where the plan has it under the couch, 52 to 73 in.
  • A combined inverter-charger with at least a 30 A pass-through, so shore power both runs the 120 V loads and charges the battery.
  • A 40 to 60 A DC-DC charger from the alternator, 4 AWG from the starter battery.
  • Light switches: by the couch, the dinette and the sink, as you asked. They all control the ceiling lights from three places, which needs momentary push-buttons into a 12 V dimming controller (a lighting module with several button inputs), not ordinary 3 way switches. I kept a small 2 gang plate at the rear doors only for the roof fan and the wet bath light.
  • Under-cabinet lights: two over the couch (a quarter and three quarters along it) and one centered over the dinette table, all on the underside of the wall cabinets. They replace the under-cabinet LED strips and the wall reading lights I had first suggested.
  • Outlets: the four 120 V outlets you asked for (couch, over the middle of the dinette table, under the TV, behind the fridge), plus one for the microwave, which cannot share a circuit with them. Everything else is 12 V or USB-C.
  • The hot water heater (1440 W) and the microwave (about 1500 W) must not run together: that is more than a 2000 W inverter can supply.
  • All 120 V work is for an electrician to check; I give layout and sizes, not a code review.

That is 1.6 times the battery on a hot day with the A/C on for 8 hours, so it needs refilling every day: driving through the alternator charger (50 A is about 640 W, so 2 hours adds about 1.3 kWh) or shore power (60 A charger, about 770 W). The fridge and the inverter idling are about a quarter of that total: a 12 V fridge would cut it, because the inverter could be switched off. These are estimates to size the system, not a measurement.

6. Order of work

  • Before the walls and ceiling are covered: run the risers, the ceiling trunk and every branch, with grommets in all steel and the 12 V and 120 V in separate looms (at least 2 in apart, crossing at right angles). This is the rough-in step of the build order.
  • With the furring strips: fix the electrical boxes to blocking between the strips, and add the blocking behind the hub board, the switch plates and the outlets before the planks go on.
  • Last: the hub board, fuses and inverter-charger, the lights and the plates, after the covering is up.
  • Test every circuit with a meter before you connect the battery, and test the GFCI outlets with the button.
