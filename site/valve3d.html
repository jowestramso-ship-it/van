The shower valve behind a small door, in 3D

The shower back wall at the cab end, seen from inside the stall (cab on the left). The back wall is one solid PVC sheet, with a small door over the valve: the valve is mounted on the door, which swings open on its cab-side hinge and shows the two swivel elbows and the lines behind it. Drag to look around, scroll or pinch to zoom, and open the door.

Drawn from the plan, with the numbers as built into it (2026-11-03): the shower back wall is the 3/8 in plywood wall on the furring rails (rails S3 to S6 at 21 1/4, 30 1/4, 43 1/2 and 57 1/4 in up, 3/4 in thick, standing 3 5/8 in off the steel on the spacer blocks; only the two spacer blocks of S4 near the valve are drawn) with ONE PVC sheet glued on it (drawn 1/8 in; the sheet is not chosen: measure yours), and the shower nook (12 to 24 in along, 38 to 54 up) cut out of both. The door does not hang on the thin PVC: the opening, 8 x 9 3/4 in at 25 1/2 to 33 1/2 in along and 32 to 41 3/4 in up, runs the whole gap between rails S4 and S5 through the plywood and the sheet, so the wall at its top and bottom edges is backed by those rails. The rear jamb is the nook frame V2 (24 to 25 1/2 in, 38 to 54 up) with a short piece VD2 under it (32 to 38 up), and the cab-side jamb VD1 (33 1/2 to 35 in, 3/4 x 1 1/2 in, screwed to both rails) carries the hinge. The door is a rigid 1/2 in board (cellular PVC, or sealed plywood), 9 x 10 3/4 in, overlapping the opening by 1/2 in all round on a 3/16 in foam gasket (it squeezes to 1/8 in), and swings open on a 6 in piano hinge between it and the sheet on the cab side, its wall leaf screwed through the sheet and the plywood into VD1. Four #8 x 2 in stainless screws at its corners hold it shut: they go through the door, the gasket, the sheet and the plywood into the wood behind. The valve is at 29 in along and 36 in up on the door, two handles with the hot and cold inlets 4 in apart. How deep the valve sits behind the door and the size of the elbows (two SharkBite UC532LFA) are my estimates: check them with your valve in hand. The cold side of the valve is the rear one (on the right here) and the hot the cab one (on the left): an assumption, check the valve. The hot line, dropping at 31 in along, passes through the S4 spacer block at 29 1/2 to 33 in, which gets a 3/4 in hole drilled through it. Leave 8 to 12 in of extra PEX behind the valve, loose, so the door can swing open on its lines, and put a shutoff on each line in the sink cabinet.
