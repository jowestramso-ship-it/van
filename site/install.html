Drawn from the sizes in the plan. I have not seen the A/C manual, and the fan has no directions: where a manual would decide something, the step says so.

Materials

  • Primer for the cut edges, rust-inhibiting, plus matching paint
  • Butyl tape and Dicor self-leveling lap sealant (or Eternabond)
  • Vehicle polyurethane adhesive: Sikaflex-252 (or 3M 5200)
  • 3/4 in plywood, Baltic birch, about a 2 x 4 ft piece, plus primer or paint for it
  • Isopropyl alcohol, rags, masking tape, paper for templates
  • Fan: 14 AWG pair, about a 5 A fuse holder, a switch, grommets
  • A/C: heavy cable, big fuse, lugs and heat shrink, as its manual says
  • Screws for the frames; the units’ own screws and gaskets

Tools

  • Drill with bits and a step bit; jigsaw with fresh fine-tooth metal blades
  • File or deburring tool, sandpaper, wire brush
  • Tape measure, square, level, pencil, magnet (to find the ribs)
  • Props or a board to hold each frame up overnight; spring clamps
  • Plastic sheet, vacuum, a blanket to protect the paint
  • Ladder, a board to kneel on across the ribs, a helper
  • Gloves, safety glasses, hearing protection, a dust mask
  • Multimeter; garden hose for the leak test

Plan for the days

TodaySteps 1 to 6: find the ribs, template, cut, deburr and prime, build and bond the frames, prop overnight. Prep the wiring.
Tomorrow morningSteps 7 and 8: dry-fit and mount the fan. Wire it.
24 to 48 hours after bondingSteps 9 to 11: A/C on, wire it, leak test.

1Find the ribs first

  • Measure from the rear-door edge (not the 6 in zero on the wall drawings). The ribs run across the van; their edge nearest the back is at 21 3/4, 37 7/8, 58 3/8, 97 3/8 and 126 3/8 in, each 2 3/4 wide.
  • Find the real ones from inside with a magnet, or by knocking, or a tiny pilot hole. Use the drawing above to see how little room there is: 1 1/2 in from the fan opening to its rib, 1 3/4 in each side of the A/C opening.
  • If the real ribs are more than about 1/2 in off these numbers, stop and tell me: the openings or frames move.

2Make the templates

  • Cut paper templates: fan 14 x 14, A/C 14 1/4 x 14 1/4 (the A/C manual may differ: the maker lists a minimum of 14.1 x 13.7).
  • Draw both diagonals and the center lines. Both diagonals must measure the same, or the template is not square.
  • Put the center of the fan template 13 1/4 in from the rear-door edge and 46 in from the driver wall; the A/C center 49 1/2 in and 34 in. Measure across horizontally (use a level or a plumb line): the roof curves.
  • Tape the template to the inside of the roof, drill a hole at each corner right through, and mark the same square on the outside from those holes.

3Cut the fan opening first, then the A/C

  • Tape the paint on both sides of each cut line with masking tape, and hang a plastic sheet under the cut to catch the steel shavings.
  • Jigsaw with a fresh fine-tooth metal blade, from corner hole to corner hole. Go slowly and keep the shoe flat; the fan is the smaller one, so it is good practice.
  • Wear glasses, gloves and hearing protection. Vacuum up every shaving: they rust and stain the roof.

4Deburr and rust-proof every cut edge

  • File or sand off the burr, inside and out. Wipe off the dust.
  • Brush rust-inhibiting primer on the cut edge and a little onto the top and bottom faces, then paint over it. Let it dry before the frame goes on.

5Build the two frames

  • 3/4 in plywood (Baltic birch is best) ripped into strips. Seal or paint all faces first, and let that dry. No pressure-treated wood: it eats steel.
  • Fan: strips 3 1/8 in wide (the depth to the finished ceiling). 2 pieces 15 1/2 long, 2 pieces 14 long fitted between them. Outside 15 1/2 square, opening 14 x 14.
  • A/C: strips 2 in wide for now (flush with the rib bottoms). 4 pieces 15 3/4 long, glued in pairs into two 1 1/2 in thick pieces; 2 pieces 14 1/4 long, 3/4 thick, fitted between them. Outside 17 1/4 along the van by 15 3/4 across.
  • The A/C frame should be 3 1/8 deep too if its manual allows about 3 1/4 in from the roof to the trim. I have not seen that manual. Check it before you decide.
  • Glue and screw the corners, then measure both diagonals: they must match. Dry-fit each frame in its opening.

6Bond the frames to the roof

  • Adhesive: one-part polyurethane made for vehicles: Sikaflex-252, or 3M 5200 (permanent). 3M 5200 Fast Cure works too: 3M lists tack-free in about 1 to 2 hours and full cure in about 48 hours, so your working time is shorter than the plain kind (check the tube). Epoxy, silicone and ordinary construction adhesive are the wrong tools.
  • Wipe the steel with isopropyl alcohol (scuff painted steel first), sand the wood face and wipe off the dust.
  • The frame’s inside faces line up with the cut edge. Lay a continuous bead, about 3/8 in wide, along the whole top edge of the frame. It is thick enough to fill the small gap where the roof arcs (up to about 1/4 in). No breaks in the bead: water finds them.
  • You have 35 minutes to press the frame up and prop it. Do one frame at a time, or have a helper. Wipe off the squeeze-out right away.
  • Prop each frame from the floor and leave it overnight. Do not screw through the roof.

Upper 60s with no rain is good: the adhesive works from 50 to 95 °F. Keep the doors open a little (humidity cures it) and the roof out of strong sun.

7Dry-fit the fan and check the screw holes

  • Lay the fan on the roof, not fastened, centered on the opening. Mark where each flange screw hole lands.
  • The flange reaches about 1 3/4 in past the opening, but the frame wall reaches only 3/4 in. Holes that fall outside the frame bite into thin steel only.
  • If so, glue a flat backing ring of 3/4 in plywood, about 1 3/4 in wide, under the roof where the holes are (keep it off the rib; clip it where needed). A Transit builder bonded wood strips for exactly this reason.

8Mount the fan (the next morning)

  • Butyl tape in a loop around the opening under the flange, set the fan, screw it down evenly.
  • Dicor self-leveling sealant over the screw heads and along the flange edge. It sits on top; the butyl tape is the real seal.
  • The fan needs 2 3/4 to 4 1/2 in from the roof to the trim. Your stack (steel + 2 in of rib + 1 1/8 in of covering) is about 3 3/16 in, so it fits with 7/16 in to spare. This only works if the finished ceiling covering is at least about 3/4 in thick.
  • Leave the trim ring until the ceiling is covered. The frame bottom is the finished-ceiling plane.

9Mount the A/C (24 to 48 hours after bonding)

  • Read its manual first for the roof thickness range and the most roof curve it allows. At the A/C the roof is not tilted, but it is at its crown and falls away about 0.9 in at each side of the unit. A flat gasket has to cover that.
  • Two people lift it: 33 lb on the maker’s sheet, 45 lb on the listing. Pad the roof and lay a board across the ribs for your feet.
  • Gasket, set it straight down on the opening, fasten as the manual says, then sealant over the fasteners.
  • The indoor panel goes on from inside after the ceiling is covered.

10Wire them

  • Fan (12 V DC): a 14 AWG pair, a fuse of about 5 A, and a switch. These are my suggestions: the fan has no amp rating beyond the listing’s 3 to 32 W (0.25 to 2.7 A).
  • A/C (12 V DC, up to about 58 A in turbo): heavy cable and a big fuse close to the battery, as its manual says. A related OutEquipPro unit lists 6 AWG for a 12 ft lead with a 100 A fuse, and 4 AWG or heavier for longer runs. That is for a different model: confirm for yours.
  • Run the wires before the walls and ceiling are covered. Use grommets or sleeves where wire goes through metal.
  • Check polarity with a meter before connecting the fan.

11Leak test, then finish

  • After the adhesive and the sealant have cured, run a garden hose over the roof from the low side up, with a helper inside with a light.
  • Any drip: dry it, find where it came from, add sealant and test again.
  • Only then insulate around the frames and cover the ceiling. The trim rings go on last.
