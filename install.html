Roof fan and air conditioner: install steps

Drawn from the sizes in the plan. I have not seen the A/C manual, and the fan has no directions: where a manual would decide something, the step says so.

Before you cut

  • A/C: I have not seen its manual. Its opening size, the roof thickness it clamps across and the most roof curve it allows decide the cut and the frame. The only figure I have is a minimum opening of 14.1 x 13.7 in. If you do not have the manual in hand, cut and frame only the fan today and leave the A/C for when you do.
  • Fan: it has no directions. This page uses what you measured: a 14 x 14 opening and 2 3/4 to 4 1/2 in from the roof to the trim.
  • Weather: cover every open hole (plastic or a board taped to clean, dry paint) whenever you stop, until its unit is on.

Materials

  • Primer for the cut edges, rust-inhibiting, plus matching paint
  • Butyl tape; non-sag lap sealant (or Eternabond tape) for the sloped fan flange; self-leveling Dicor only on the flat
  • Vehicle polyurethane adhesive: Sikaflex-252 (or 3M 5200)
  • 3/4 in plywood, Baltic birch, about a 2 x 4 ft piece, wood glue, plus primer or paint for it
  • Isopropyl alcohol, rags, masking tape, paper for templates, plastic sheeting and painter’s tape to cover the holes
  • Fan: 14 AWG pair, about a 5 A fuse holder, a switch, grommets
  • A/C: heavy cable, big fuse, lugs and heat shrink, as its manual says
  • Screws for the frames; the units’ own screws and gaskets

Tools

  • Drill with bits and a step bit; jigsaw with fresh fine-tooth metal blades
  • File or deburring tool, sandpaper, wire brush; a saw to rip the strips; a caulk gun
  • Tape measure, square, level, straightedge, pencil, magnet (to find the ribs)
  • Props or a board to hold each frame up overnight; spring clamps
  • Plastic sheet, vacuum, a blanket to protect the paint
  • Ladder, a board to kneel on across the ribs, a helper
  • Gloves, safety glasses, hearing protection, a dust mask
  • Multimeter; garden hose for the leak test

Also on your list: the storage nook back (a separate job from the roof)

  • OSI Quad Max (paintable hybrid sealant) for a thin bead where the plywood meets the wall covering; leave the top or bottom edge unsealed so the wool can breathe
  • Havelock wool for the nook: a pad cut from a batt, about 7/8 in thick when fitted (split the batt into layers if it is thicker), cut about 1/4 in oversize so it stays put
  • 3/8 in plywood for the back of each nook, two pieces (cut each about 17 1/8 x 15 7/8: both the jar nook, 76 to 93 1/4 in from the back, and the shower nook, 12 1/2 to 29 3/4, are 17 1/4 x 16, 38 to 54 in up). With 1 1/4 in spare behind the jars, 3/8 in plywood leaves 7/8 in for the wool
  • Water-based polyurethane for the back and every edge of that plywood, two coats, cured for a few days before it goes in
  • Isopropyl alcohol and rags to wipe the steel, touch-up white paint and rust-inhibiting primer for any chips in the factory paint

Plan for the days

TodaySteps 1 to 7: find the ribs, template, cut (the A/C only with its manual), deburr and prime, mark the fan’s screw holes, build and bond the frames, prop overnight, cover the holes. Prep the wiring.
Tomorrow morningStep 8: mount the fan, then wire it.
About 48 hours after bondingSteps 9 to 11: A/C on, wire it, leak test.

1Find the ribs first

  • Measure from the rear-door edge (not the 6 in zero on the wall drawings). The ribs run across the van; their edge nearest the back is at 21 3/4, 37 7/8, 58 3/8, 97 3/8 and 126 3/8 in, each 2 3/4 wide.
  • Find the real ones from inside with a magnet, or by knocking. Measure the rib edges to 1/8 in: the drawing above shows how little room there is, 1 1/2 in from the fan opening to its rib and 1 3/4 in each side of the A/C opening, and the A/C frame leaves only 1/4 in to each rib.
  • If a rib edge next to the A/C is more than 1/8 in closer than 40 5/8 or 58 3/8 in, trim the 1 1/2 in rib-side pieces in step 6 before you glue. If any rib is more than about 1/2 in away from the numbers, stop and tell me: the openings or frames move.

2Make the templates

  • Cut paper templates: fan 14 x 14, A/C 14 1/4 x 14 1/4 (the A/C manual may say otherwise: the maker lists a minimum of 14.1 x 13.7).
  • Draw both diagonals and the center lines. Both diagonals must measure the same, or the template is not square.
  • Put the center of the fan template 13 1/4 in from the rear-door edge and 34 in from the driver wall (the middle of the van); the A/C center 49 1/2 in and 34 in. Measure across horizontally (use a level or a plumb line): the roof curves.
  • Tape the template to the inside of the roof, drill a hole at each corner right through, and mark the same square on the outside from those holes. Seal any hole you do not cut out with primer and sealant.

3Cut the fan opening first, then the A/C

  • A/C only if you have its manual’s opening size. Without it, cut the fan today and leave the A/C hole for later.
  • Tape the paint on both sides of each cut line with masking tape, and hang a plastic sheet under the cut to catch the steel shavings.
  • Jigsaw with a fresh fine-tooth metal blade, from corner hole to corner hole. Go slowly and keep the shoe flat; the fan is the smaller one, so it is good practice.
  • Wear glasses, gloves and hearing protection. Vacuum up every shaving: they rust and stain the roof.
  • Cover each hole with plastic or a board taped to clean, dry paint whenever you stop, until its unit is on.

4Deburr and rust-proof every cut edge

  • File or sand off the burr, inside and out. Wipe off the dust.
  • Brush rust-inhibiting primer on the cut edge and a little onto the top and bottom faces, then paint over it. Keep the paint to the edge and a thin band: the frame bonds to the underside of the roof next to the cut, and that band is scuffed and cleaned bare in step 7.
  • Let the primer and paint dry as the cans say before the frame goes on. Do the cutting early in the day.

5Lay the fan on the roof and mark its screw holes

  • Set the fan on the roof unfastened, centered on the opening, and mark where each flange screw hole lands. Do this before you build the frame, so the frame and backing rings come out right.
  • The flange reaches about 1 3/4 in past the opening, but the frame wall reaches only 3/4 in. A hole that falls outside the frame bites into thin steel.
  • If so, add a backing ring: 3/4 in plywood strips, 1 in wide, butted to the outside of the frame wall under the roof (outer edge 8 3/4 in from the fan’s center), and only 1/2 in wide on the rib side so it stays off the rib. It is bonded with the frame in step 7, so it is cured before the fan screws go in. One Transit build on the Sportsmobile forum bonded wood strips for the same reason.
  • The fan sits in the middle of the van, at the crown of the roof arc, so the flange falls away about 3/8 in below its highest point across its 17.7 in base. See step 8.

6Build the two frames

  • 3/4 in plywood (Baltic birch is best) ripped into strips. No pressure-treated wood: it eats steel. Cut, glue and screw the frame first, laminate the thick A/C pieces, then seal or paint the finished frame, leaving the top face bare or sanded for the adhesive.
  • Fan: strips 2 15/16 in wide (the depth to the finished ceiling: the rib’s 2 in plus the 15/16 in covering). 2 pieces 15 1/2 long, 2 pieces 14 long fitted between them. Outside 15 1/2 square, opening 14 x 14. Cut the backing ring pieces from step 5 too.
  • A/C: strips 2 in wide for now (flush with the rib bottoms). 4 pieces 15 3/4 long, glued in pairs into two 1 1/2 in thick pieces; 2 pieces 14 1/4 long, 3/4 thick, fitted between them. Outside 17 1/4 along the van by 15 3/4 across.
  • Make the A/C frame 2 15/16 deep too if its manual allows about 3 in from the roof to the trim. I have not seen that manual. Check it before you decide.
  • Measure both diagonals of each frame: they must match. Hold each frame up under the roof around its opening and check that its inside faces line up with the cut and that it clears the ribs.

7Bond the frames to the roof

  • Adhesive: one-part polyurethane made for vehicles: Sikaflex-252, or 3M 5200 (permanent). 3M 5200 Fast Cure works too: 3M lists tack-free in about 1 to 2 hours and full cure in about 48 hours, so your working time is shorter than the plain kind (check the tube). Epoxy, silicone and ordinary construction adhesive are the wrong tools.
  • Wipe the steel band next to the cut with isopropyl alcohol, scuff it, and wipe again. Sand the wood face and wipe off the dust.
  • The frame’s inside faces line up with the cut edge. Lay a continuous bead, about 3/8 in wide, along the whole top edge of the frame (and the backing rings). No breaks in the bead: water finds them.
  • Both openings sit in the middle of the van, at the crown of the roof, so the frames go on level: no tilt. The arc still leaves up to about 1/4 in of gap under a frame (the top face is straight, the roof is curved). Check with a straightedge that the top face touches the steel along its length, and let the bead fill what is left.
  • You have 35 minutes (Sikaflex-252) to press the frame up and prop it. Do one frame at a time, or have a helper. Wipe off the squeeze-out right away.
  • Prop each frame from the floor and leave it overnight. Do not screw through the roof. Cover each hole with plastic taped over it.

Upper 60s with no rain is good: the adhesive works from 50 to 95 °F. Keep the doors open a little (humidity cures it) and the roof out of strong sun.

8Mount the fan (the next morning)

  • Butyl tape in a loop around the opening under the flange, set the fan, screw it down evenly into the frame and the backing rings.
  • Use a non-sag lap sealant (or Eternabond tape) over the screw heads and the flange edge: it stays put on the curve. The butyl tape is the real seal.
  • The roof falls away about 3/8 in below its highest point across the 17.7 in flange. Check how much curve the fan allows; if it will not seat, use a shaped or thick foam gasket or a curb.
  • The fan needs 2 3/4 to 4 1/2 in from the roof to the trim. Your stack (steel + 2 in of rib + 15/16 in of covering: 3/16 in plywood, then 3/4 in slats) is about 3 in, so it fits with 1/4 in to spare above the minimum. This only works if the finished ceiling covering is at least about 11/16 in thick.
  • Leave the trim ring until the ceiling is covered. The frame bottom is the finished-ceiling plane.

9Mount the A/C (48 hours after bonding)

  • Read its manual first for the opening size, the roof thickness range and the most roof curve it allows. At the A/C the roof is not tilted, but it is at its crown and falls away about 0.9 in at each side of the unit. A flat gasket cannot close that: use a shaped gasket or curb, whatever the manual allows.
  • Two people lift it: 33 lb on the maker’s sheet, 45 lb on the listing. Pad the roof and lay a board across the ribs for your feet.
  • Gasket, set it straight down on the opening, fasten as the manual says, then non-sag sealant over the fasteners.
  • The indoor panel goes on from inside after the ceiling is covered.

10Wire them

  • Fan (12 V DC): a 14 AWG pair, a fuse of about 5 A, and a switch. These are my suggestions: the fan has no amp rating beyond the listing’s 3 to 32 W (0.25 to 2.7 A).
  • A/C (12 V DC, up to about 58 A in turbo): heavy cable and a big fuse close to the battery, as its manual says. A related OutEquipPro unit lists 6 AWG for a 12 ft lead with a 100 A fuse, and 4 AWG or heavier for longer runs. That is for a different model: confirm for yours.
  • Run the wires before the walls and ceiling are covered. Use grommets or sleeves where wire goes through metal.
  • Check polarity with a meter before connecting the fan.

11Leak test, then finish

  • After the adhesive and the sealant have cured, run a garden hose over the roof from the low side up, with a helper inside with a light.
  • Any drip: dry it, find where it came from, add sealant and test again.
  • Only then insulate around the frames and cover the ceiling. The trim rings go on last.
