Hot water is electric (latest, user: 'hot water is electric'): the hot water tank under the sink (13 x 12.5 x 18.5 tall) is an electric heater, so there is no vent, flue or combustion air to plan for and the cabinet next to the sliding door is fine (a CO alarm is still good practice, but not because of this heater). New items: (1) voltage and wattage not chosen: a typical RV electric heater is 120 V AC at about 1,000 to 1,440 W for roughly 6 gal, which needs an inverter of 1,500 W or more (the microwave plan already assumed about 2,000 W; do not run both at once), or shore power; a 12 V DC element is much smaller, about 300 to 500 W; (2) energy: heating 6 gal by about 80 degrees F takes about 1.2 kWh, roughly 90 to 100 Ah of a 12 V battery through an inverter, so check the battery capacity (21 x 10 x 9.5 in, capacity not noted) and plan to heat on shore power, or from the engine alternator through a DC-DC charger and inverter, or with solar; (3) its own breaker or GFCI circuit and the right wire gauge (1,440 W is about 12 A: 14 AWG minimum on 15 A, 12 AWG for 20 A); (4) the power cable runs from the battery and inverter on the driver side (52 to 73 from the back) across to the sink cabinet (109 to 122) on the passenger side, and the 1 3/4 in strip behind the tank already has to carry the drain and supply pipes, so find a separate path for the cable; (5) it still needs a pressure and temperature relief valve piped to a safe drain, a winterizing drain, and the clearance to wood the manual requires (the tank surface is warm, about 140 F water); (6) where the inverter goes is not decided.
